**Mérida, Yucatán, November 19, 2026.** — With the aim of strengthening the development of language technologies for Mexico's Indigenous languages, graduate students Fátima Miranda Pestaña and Jaziel A. Carballo Tadeo, from the Centro de Investigación en Ciencias de Información Geoespacial (CentroGeo), Mérida campus, gave an oral presentation at [CICY SUMMIT 2026: Regional Congress of Graduate Students](/event/cicy-summit-2026/).

The scientific meeting, organized by the Centro de Investigación Científica de Yucatán (CICY), took place on November 18 and 19, 2026, in CICY's Main Auditorium and Multipurpose Room (SUM) in Mérida, Yucatán, bringing together young researchers and specialists from a range of scientific and technological disciplines across southeastern Mexico.

### Reliable data to teach AI to listen to Maya

Under the title “Reliable Data to Teach AI to Listen to Maya,” the talk, developed under the supervision of Dr. Alejandro Molina Villegas, addressed one of today's main technological challenges: training Automatic Speech Recognition (ASR) systems for languages with scarce digital resources.

{{< figure src="ponencia-jaziel.jpg" caption="Jaziel A. Carballo Tadeo presents the current landscape of automatic speech recognition." >}}

### A systematic audit of the T'aantsil corpus

The research presented is part of both students' master's and doctoral projects, which aim to integrate a speech recognition model into [T'aantsil](https://taantsil.com.mx), a digital platform hosting a linguistic corpus documented in communities across the Yucatán Peninsula.

To ensure data quality before fine-tuning the acoustic models, the team designed and implemented a three-cycle supervised audit methodology over the corpus's audio segments. By combining computational indicators (speech rate and Maya–Spanish bilingual text parity) with human listening review, without relying on a prior acoustic model, the procedure made it possible to detect and clean up empty, incomplete, misaligned, or duplicate segments, consolidating a cleaned corpus ready for training AI models.

### Scientific impact and language preservation

The participation of this CentroGeo team in CICY SUMMIT 2026 highlights the importance of applying data science and artificial intelligence with methodological rigor and cultural relevance. This work lays the technical groundwork for automatic transcription tools to contribute to the documentation, accessibility, and digital preservation of the Yucatec Maya language.
