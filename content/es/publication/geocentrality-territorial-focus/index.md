abstract: This paper introduces and evaluates the concept of geocentrality, defined as the degree to which a scientific document places geographical territory at the center of its thematic, methodological, or analytical structure. Using a pilot dataset of documents annotated by four independent reviewers, we analyze inter-annotator agreement and validate an automatic geocentrality metric. Results show moderate to good agreement across annotators and strong correlations between human labels and the automated geocentrality score. Based on error analysis, we propose revised decision thresholds and an improved annotation protocol. These findings provide an empirical foundation for computational approaches to territorial centrality in text, with applications to spatial information retrieval and geographically grounded document exploration.
